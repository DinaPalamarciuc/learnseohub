AI Mode & AI Overviews: More Links, Source Preferences, News Carousels
AI SearchOct 15, 2026
Google announced a wave of updates to AI Mode and AI Overviews: links now appear higher in responses and more frequently (Google reports "continued and substantial increases"); a new dedicated section at the top of some AI responses highlights 1–2 official pages for a task; news carousels are expanding to more regions and explicitly highlight Preferred Sources; and article links at the end of responses now include image thumbnails. Additionally, Preferred Sources use has grown more than 7× since January 2026 — a signal Google cannot ignore.
Your action: Make sure your homepage and key landing pages present themselves as "official sources" for your topic. Strong hero images + consistent branded publishing cadence are now ranking signals inside AI surfaces.
Source Preferences Button: Users Can Now Manage Preferred Sources Inside AI Mode
AI SearchOct 2026
Google is testing a "Source preferences" button directly inside the AI Mode citations sidebar. Users can add or remove Preferred Sources without leaving the SERP. This deepens the importance of brand recognition: if your audience doesn't know your site name, they won't add you — and they won't see you cited as often.
Your action: Invest in audience retention — email lists, social presence, repeat visits. Brand familiarity is now a direct input to AI Search visibility.
Google Testing AI-Written Citation Summaries in AI Overviews
AI SearchOct 2026
Google is testing AI-generated descriptions in AI Overview citation cards. The text is summarized for the specific query — site owners cannot directly control it, similar to how meta descriptions are sometimes rewritten. The implication: on-page clarity and structure matter more than ever, because Google uses them to construct your citation snippet.
Your action: Front-load your most important claim or answer in the first 2–3 sentences of each section. Structured, clear prose gives Google better material to summarize from.
October 2026
September 2026 Spam Update Completes — Longest Rollout Since Aug 2025
AlgorithmSept 24 – Oct 8, 2026
The September 2026 spam update ran 13 days and 16 hours — the longest in over a year. Google described it as "a normal spam update" with no new policy, but noted it is running more frequent spam updates because of growing content volumes and that AI is now being used to catch more spam. Scaled content was flagged as a bigger current problem than link spam.
Your action: Audit your site for thin, templated, or bulk-AI content. Each page should have a clear editorial reason to exist and add something beyond what's already ranking.
UGC Fresh Data Program — Google Opens Applications for Forum & UGC Sites
TechnicalOct 8, 2026
Google published documentation for the UGC Fresh Data Program, which lets approved forum and user-generated content platforms push fresh content to Google outside the normal crawl queue and Indexing API. Applications get a response in 6–8 weeks. This is significant for community-driven sites that need fast indexing of user-submitted posts.
Your action: If you run a forum, Q&A section, or UGC-heavy site, review the program criteria and apply. Faster indexing of fresh community content is a direct competitive advantage.
Google Updates Authorship Guidance: Fake Authors and AI Headshots Are Now a Risk
E-E-A-TOct 6, 2026
Google updated its authorship guidance to explicitly warn against deceptive author information — including AI-generated headshots, invented names, and false credentials. This reverses an earlier Google stance that it "wasn't worried" about fake author signals. Combined with the spam update's focus on scaled content, the direction is clear: Google is tightening its grip on trust signals.
Your action: Audit every author page and byline on your site. Real names, real photos, verifiable credentials, and linking to social profiles are now mandatory for YMYL content and increasingly important everywhere else.
AI Content Now Requires Manual Fact-Checking, Google Guidance Confirms
Content QualityOct 1, 2026
Google updated its generative AI content guidance to state that it is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content" before publishing. This is the clearest official statement yet that AI-first workflows without human editorial oversight are a risk. The helpful content page was also updated to say raters judge content on effort, originality, talent or skill, and accuracy.
Your action: Build a human review step into every AI-assisted content workflow. Treat AI output as a first draft, not a final product.
Search Console AI Performance Reports Now Available for All Sites
ToolsAug–Oct 2026
Following the June 2026 launch, Google's Search Generative AI performance reports in Search Console are now widely available. The reports show clicks and impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode separately from traditional Search. This is the first time publishers can see direct traffic attribution from AI-generated results.
Your action: Check Search Console → Search results → filter by "AI Overviews" or "AI Mode" to see your current AI-driven traffic. Use this as your baseline before making any GEO optimizations.
